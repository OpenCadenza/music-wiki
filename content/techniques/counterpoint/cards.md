# Counterpoint and fugue cards

## id: `counterpoint.cantus-firmus`

- type: procedure
- facets: counterpoint, cantus firmus, melody, exercise
- sources: omt-cantus-firmus
- confidence: established
- scope: Strict species exercises
- signals: The fixed line has a clear tonal center, singable motion, controlled
  range, one well-placed climax, and a stable final arrival.
- counterexample: A random collection of consonant notes is not a cantus firmus.
- limits: Its specific contour rules are pedagogical and should not be applied
  mechanically to free composition.

Write and preserve the cantus before adding counterpoint. Every later claim
refers to those actual notes.

## id: `counterpoint.species-purpose`

- type: concept
- facets: counterpoint, species, learning, independence
- sources: omt-species-intro
- confidence: established
- scope: Two-voice species pedagogy
- signals: Each exercise targets a new rhythmic or dissonance problem while
  maintaining melodic independence and coordinated vertical intervals.
- counterexample: Passing a species exercise does not prove competence in all
  historical counterpoint.
- limits: The method is a controlled teaching model, not a complete style ruleset.

Use species cards as controlled experiments. State which principle a real score
task is testing.

## id: `counterpoint.first-species`

- type: procedure
- facets: counterpoint, first-species, intervals, parallel, hidden
- sources: omt-first-species
- confidence: established
- scope: One note against one note in strict tonal species
- signals: Opening and ending use suitable perfect consonances; motion is varied;
  perfect fifths and octaves are not repeated in parallel; approach to perfect
  consonances is checked.
- counterexample: Repeating a perfect fifth at a compound interval is still a
  repeated perfect fifth type.
- limits: Similar motion into a perfect interval is not forbidden in every
  historical or modern style.

Inspect each vertical onset and label the motion. Escalate style context before
generalizing an exercise prohibition.

## id: `counterpoint.second-species`

- type: procedure
- facets: counterpoint, second-species, passing, neighbor, weak beat
- sources: omt-second-species
- confidence: established
- scope: Two notes against one cantus note
- signals: Weak-beat dissonances function as controlled passing or neighbor
  tones; strong-beat consonance and cross-bar voice motion remain coordinated.
- counterexample: Any dissonant weak beat is not automatically a passing tone.
- limits: Free composition allows more complex rhythmic levels.

Label the preparation, dissonance role, and resolution for each disputed event.

## id: `counterpoint.third-species`

- type: procedure
- facets: counterpoint, third-species, florid, cambiata
- sources: omt-third-species
- confidence: established
- scope: Four notes against one cantus note
- signals: The line remains singable and varied; strong-beat consonance anchors
  local figuration; exceptions such as cambiata are identified rather than
  treated as accidental rules.
- counterexample: Faster surface rhythm does not prove independent counterpoint.
- limits: The exercise's rhythmic ratio is not a general texture specification.

Trace each strong beat before adding ornaments to a simpler scheme.

## id: `counterpoint.suspension`

- type: procedure
- facets: counterpoint, fourth-species, suspension, preparation, resolution
- sources: omt-fourth-species
- confidence: established
- scope: Strict fourth species and contextual suspension analysis
- signals: A consonant preparation is tied into a dissonant strong beat and
  resolves according to the specified interval pattern.
- counterexample: A tied note is not a suspension unless its vertical function
  creates a controlled dissonance with an actual resolution.
- limits: Decorative retardation, chordal sevenths, and later styles need
  contextual labels.

Name preparation, suspension, and resolution with exact pitches and voices.

## id: `counterpoint.free-counterpoint-evidence`

- type: procedure
- facets: counterpoint, free, voice leading, style, evidence
- sources: omt-first-species, omt-fugue
- confidence: established
- scope: Reviewing free tonal textures
- signals: Horizontal lines remain identifiable; vertical dissonances have local
  roles; voice crossings, doubled perfect intervals, and simultaneous attacks are
  interpreted in context.
- counterexample: A parallel-fifth candidate does not automatically condemn
  Impressionist planing or a doubled melodic line.
- limits: A rule from one style needs a scope and a counterexample.

Report exact voices, pitches, and onset times before naming an infraction.

## id: `fugue.subject`

- type: procedure
- facets: fugue, subject, motive, answer space, tonal response
- sources: omt-fugue, umn-composing-fugue
- confidence: established
- scope: Baroque-style fugue planning
- signals: A singable subject has a recognizable head and tail, enough identity to
  support recurrence, and prior tests for answer and countersubject combinations.
- counterexample: A long melody with no memorable head is weak fugue material.
- limits: Historical subjects vary widely; no single contour or length is
  mandatory.

Preserve the subject's native notes. Trial its answer and combination before
composing the exposition.

## id: `fugue.answer`

- type: procedure
- facets: fugue, answer, real, tonal, dominant, mutation
- sources: omt-fugue, umn-composing-fugue
- confidence: established
- scope: Tonal fugue subject response
- signals: The answer is identified as real or tonal; any tonal mutation is
  explained by the subject's interval structure and the home-key boundary.
- counterexample: Literal transposition and tonal answer are both possible;
  one is not universally correct.
- limits: An answer can be altered for counterpoint as well as tonal reasons.

Compare subject and answer pitch by pitch. State the key and the reason for each
mutation.

## id: `fugue.countersubject`

- type: procedure
- facets: fugue, countersubject, invertible, combination
- sources: omt-fugue, umn-composing-fugue
- confidence: established
- scope: Repeated accompanying material in a fugue
- signals: The line recurs with multiple subject or answer statements and can be
  tested in its actual above or below orientation.
- counterexample: Initial free counterpoint is not yet proven to be a regular
  countersubject.
- limits: Many fugues use no regular countersubject.

Do not label initial accompaniment as a countersubject without repeated evidence.
Trial the intended combination in both voice orders.

## id: `fugue.exposition`

- type: procedure
- facets: fugue, exposition, entry, order, subject, answer
- sources: omt-fugue, umn-composing-fugue
- confidence: established
- scope: Fugal exposition
- signals: Every intended voice presents subject or answer; entry order, tonal
  direction, and overlap with existing lines are explicit.
- counterexample: A fixed subject-answer ordering does not apply to all fugues.
- limits: Incomplete and irregular expositions occur.

Record entrants, voices, pitches, and onset bars. Check overlap rather than only
the labels.

## id: `fugue.episode`

- type: procedure
- facets: fugue, episode, sequence, modulation, free material
- sources: omt-fugue, umn-composing-fugue
- confidence: established
- scope: Material between subject statements
- signals: An episode develops subject material or introduces purposeful free
  material and changes key or texture enough to prepare later entries.
- counterexample: Filling space with unrelated scales does not automatically
  create a convincing episode.
- limits: Some historical fugues avoid episodes.

Give each episode a material source, harmonic direction, and reason to end.

## id: `fugue.stretto-and-false-entry`

- type: procedure
- facets: fugue, stretto, false entry, overlap, climax
- sources: omt-fugue
- confidence: provisional
- scope: Advanced fugal development
- signals: Stretto overlaps actual subject material at defined entries; a false
  entry intentionally uses only enough head material to create expectation
  before changing course.
- counterexample: A similar phrase in another voice does not automatically
  constitute stretto.
- limits: Terminology differs by theory and repertoire.

Trial the exact entry spacing and combination first. Label a false entry only
when the withheld continuation is audible in the score relation.
